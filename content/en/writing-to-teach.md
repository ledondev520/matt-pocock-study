## Give readers a reason

An explanation promises a powerful, flexible, efficient system. Readers still cannot decide what to do. Start with a specific puzzle instead: why does the interface claim success after saving fails? Use a real problem without inventing a personal story.

## Give the concept somewhere to land

Describe the consequence, explain its cause, introduce a method and test it in one small situation. A before-and-after comparison makes the change visible. Introduce one example for each concept before adding variations.

## Make sentences do work

A concrete verb can point to behaviour. Preserve the input after validation fails gives readers something to implement and inspect. A claim about a transformed experience leaves the action unspecified.

<!-- compare -->
| Wording | What a reader can do |
| --- | --- |
| Use robust validation | Guess where to start |
| Name the invalid field and preserve the input | Implement and inspect one behaviour |

Short sentences, space and comparisons help readers follow the idea. British slang, profanity and particular punctuation choices are not requirements for good teaching. Remove sentences that change no action, then check that the necessary limits remain clear.

Inspect the example’s input and expected outcome separately. Without an input, readers cannot identify the situation; without an expected outcome, they cannot check an answer. Preserve the information needed for the decision before removing decorative wording.

## Close the explanation

```cards
- **Retrieval** Close the explanation and answer unaided.
- **Spacing** Return later and record what you forgot.
- **Interleaving** Mix related problems that require different choices.
```

Teach one small concept, then ask for a decision. Define completion as handling an unfamiliar input. Reading smoothly is insufficient evidence.

## Check the teaching too

AI explanations can contain factual errors. Keep reliable sources for key claims and have a person check examples and answers. Correlations involving likes or bookmarks in an archived sample do not establish that a writing technique causes learning.

```note
kind: inference
Our suggestion: separate checkable facts, example assumptions and application advice. Verify facts against sources, inspect example behaviour and state the conditions attached to advice.
```

## Exercise: replace the adjectives

```exercise
title: Turn a claim into a lesson
- Draft: our import feature is powerful, intelligent and easy.
- Task: provide a reader problem, one method and an observable result.
- Variation: replace a missing name with a duplicate identifier.
answer: One row lacks a name: return its row number and field, then allow a corrected import. Duplicate identifiers require identifying the conflicting rows; the missing-field answer is insufficient.
```

Done means another reader can predict the feedback from your explanation and justify an answer to the variation. Continue with [feedback and attribution](/en/feedback-and-attribution) to improve the lesson.

## Quick reference

```cards
- **Opening** Name a specific difficulty or goal.
- **Development** Problem, cause, method, concrete example.
- **Completion** Solve a fresh problem and check behaviour and sources.
```
